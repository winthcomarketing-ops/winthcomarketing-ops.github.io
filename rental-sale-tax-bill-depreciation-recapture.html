Rental Sale Tax Bill: The Code Sections Behind Depreciation Recapture

Published October 18, 2026 · 721 UPREIT Pros

The tax on selling a rental often surprises owners because several provisions of the Internal Revenue Code apply at once. This short reference names each one and what it generally does.

Section 167 and 168: depreciation

Residential rental buildings are generally depreciated over 27.5 years and commercial buildings over 39 years. Land is not depreciated. Each deduction lowers adjusted basis under Section 1016, including depreciation that was allowable but not claimed.

Section 1250 and Section 1(h)(6): the 25% layer

For most real estate, straight-line depreciation is recovered on sale as unrecaptured Section 1250 gain, taxed at a maximum federal rate of 25% (see IRS Topic 409). This page details depreciation recapture on a rental property sale.

Section 1(h): long-term capital gain

Remaining gain on property held more than one year is generally taxed at 0%, 15% or 20%.

Section 1411: the 3.8% surtax

The net investment income tax may apply to gain above income thresholds.

Section 1031 and Rev. Rul. 2004-86: deferral

A like-kind exchange can generally defer the full gain, and DST interests can qualify as replacement property. See the benefits of a 1031 exchange and DST 1031 planning before listing a rental.

Section 721: contribution for OP units

Contributing property to an operating partnership for units is generally tax-deferred, with basis carried over. This guide covers tax-deferred options when you stop being a landlord.
