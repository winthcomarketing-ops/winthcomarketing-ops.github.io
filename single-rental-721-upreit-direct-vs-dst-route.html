Single Rental to OP Units: Direct Contribution vs. the 1031 and DST Route

Published October 19, 2026 · 721 UPREIT Pros

This page compares the two ways an owner of one rental property might eventually hold OP units in a REIT's operating partnership.

Route A: direct 721 contribution

  • Governing rule: Section 721, which generally allows a tax-deferred contribution of property to a partnership.
  • Who decides: the operating partnership, which must accept the specific property.
  • Typical fit: larger, institutional-quality assets in the REIT's target sector and markets.
  • Watch for: debt relief and other partnership rules that can trigger gain; see what a direct property to UPREIT acceptance checklist asks for.

For one rental house, Route A is usually a long shot. This page on real estate that qualifies for a 721 UPREIT contribution explains why.

Route B: 1031 exchange into a DST, then a possible 721

  • Step 1: sell the rental through a qualified intermediary under Section 1031, identifying replacements within 45 days and closing within 180 days.
  • Step 2: buy beneficial interests in a Delaware Statutory Trust that holds like-kind real estate.
  • Step 3: if the sponsor later contributes the DST property to an UPREIT, interests may convert to OP units under Section 721. This is at the sponsor's discretion.
  • Trade-off: you give up control and liquidity, as outlined in this comparison of DSTs versus owning rental property.

What both routes share

Neither eliminates tax. Deferred gain generally rides into the OP units and can be recognized on redemption or sale. Once you hold OP units, a further 1031 exchange of that position generally isn't available. Review 1031 DST and 721 UPREIT guidance with your CPA and attorney.
