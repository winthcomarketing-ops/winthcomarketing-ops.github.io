Is a 721 UPREIT Tax-Free? A Technical Look at What the Code Actually Defers

Published October 16, 2026 · 721 UPREIT Pros

Section 721 is a nonrecognition rule, not an exclusion. This note traces where the deferred gain sits after a contribution to a REIT operating partnership and which Code provisions can bring it back into income.

Nonrecognition and carryover basis

  • Section 721(a): no gain or loss is recognized when property is contributed to a partnership for a partnership interest.
  • Section 722: the partner's outside basis in the OP units equals the adjusted basis of the property contributed, reduced for any net liability relief.
  • Section 723: the partnership takes the same inside basis in the property.

Because both bases carry over, the built-in gain is preserved twice, once in the units and once in the asset. For a primer, see this 721 UPREIT tax-deferred exchange overview.

Provisions that can trigger recognition

  • Section 704(c): pre-contribution gain is allocated to the contributing partner when the partnership sells the property. Contractual protection is reviewed in this 721 UPREIT tax protection agreement coverage checklist.
  • Section 752(b): a decrease in the partner's share of liabilities is a deemed cash distribution.
  • Section 731(a): money distributed in excess of outside basis is recognized as gain.
  • Section 707(a)(2)(B): transfers of money within two years of a contribution are presumed to be a disguised sale.
  • Redemption of OP units for cash or exchange for REIT shares is generally a taxable disposition.

Character and rate

Recognized gain attributable to prior straight-line depreciation is unrecaptured Section 1250 gain, taxed at a maximum 25% rate under Section 1(h). The remainder is generally long-term capital gain at up to 20%. Section 1411 can add 3.8%. Prior 1031 deferral through a DST lowers basis further; see DST 1031 depreciation recapture and basis.

Death

Under current Section 1014, inherited OP units generally receive a basis equal to fair market value at death. Details on exits, debt and basis are in this 721 exchange tax guide.
