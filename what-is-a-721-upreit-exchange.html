What Is a 721 UPREIT Exchange? An IRC Section 721 Overview for Investors

Published October 14, 2026 · 721 UPREIT Pros

A 721 UPREIT exchange combines a specific Internal Revenue Code provision with a specific REIT structure. This overview is for investors who want the mechanics, not just the headline.

The statute: IRC Section 721(a)

Section 721(a) provides that no gain or loss is recognized by a partnership or its partners when property is contributed in exchange for an interest in the partnership. Two companion rules matter. Under Section 722, your basis in the partnership interest generally equals your adjusted basis in the contributed property. Under Section 723, the partnership takes that same carryover basis. Deferral, in other words, travels with the units.

The structure: the umbrella partnership REIT

In an UPREIT, the REIT holds most of its real estate through an operating partnership (OP). Contributors receive OP units, which are often redeemable for cash or REIT shares, at the REIT's election, after a holding period. Where gain can still appear

  • Liability relief: under Sections 752 and 731, a drop in your share of debt that exceeds basis may be treated as a cash distribution.
  • Disguised sale rules (Section 707): cash or certain distributions near the contribution can recharacterize part of it as a sale.
  • Built-in gain (Section 704(c)): if the OP sells contributed property, pre-contribution gain is generally allocated back to the contributor, which is why tax protection agreements exist.
  • Redemption: converting OP units to cash or shares is generally a taxable event.

Interaction with Section 1031

Since 2018, Section 1031 applies only to real property, and partnership interests are not real property, so OP units generally cannot be exchanged into new real estate. Research another 1031 exchange after a 721 contribution and 1031 exchange planning after a 721 UPREIT before committing. If you reach an UPREIT through a DST, the first leg still follows the 1031 exchange identification and closing deadlines.
