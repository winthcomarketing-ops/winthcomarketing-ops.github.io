Section 1031 vs Section 721: A Technical Comparison for Real Estate Investors

Published October 15, 2026 · 721 UPREIT Pros

Both provisions defer gain, but they rest on different parts of the Internal Revenue Code and carry different follow-on rules. This overview compares them at the statute level.

The operative rules

  • Section 1031(a): no gain or loss is recognized on an exchange of real property held for productive use in a trade or business or for investment, if exchanged solely for like-kind real property held the same way. Since 2018 it applies only to real property.
  • Section 721(a): no gain or loss is recognized by a partnership or its partners when property is contributed in exchange for an interest in the partnership.

Timing

Section 1031(a)(3) requires a deferred exchange to identify replacement property within 45 days and complete the exchange within 180 days. Section 721 has no equivalent window because a contribution is not a sale. When a DST sits between the two, the 1031 timing applies to the first leg; see how the 1031, DST and 721 pathway works.

Basis

Under Section 1031(d), basis in replacement property generally carries over from the relinquished property, adjusted for boot. Under Sections 722 and 723, the contributor's basis in OP units equals the contributed property's basis, and the partnership takes that same basis in the property.

Where gain can surface

  • 1031: cash or other non-like-kind property (boot) under Section 1031(b), including net debt relief.
  • 721: liability shifts under Section 752, disguised sale rules under Section 707, and built-in gain allocations under Section 704(c) if the partnership sells contributed property.

Exchanging again

Because partnership interests are not real property, OP units generally cannot be relinquished in a later 1031.
