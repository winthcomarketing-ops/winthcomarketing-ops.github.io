Form 1065, Schedule K-1 and Outside Basis After a Section 721 Contribution

A qualifying contribution of property to a partnership under IRC Section 721 is generally a nonrecognition event, but it changes the investor's annual reporting from direct property ownership to partnership reporting. This page summarizes the technical pieces.

Who files what

  • Operating partnership: files Form 1065 and issues each partner a Schedule K-1 (and K-2/K-3 where relevant).
  • OP unit holder: reports allocated items and maintains an outside-basis record.
  • REIT shareholder (after any conversion): receives Form 1099-DIV.

Outside basis is your job

The K-1 capital account is not tax basis. Outside basis starts with the carryover basis of the contributed property, adjusted under Section 752 for your share of partnership liabilities, then for income, deductions and distributions. A decrease in liability share is treated as a distribution of money and can produce gain if it exceeds basis.

Section 704(c) and allocated income

Built-in gain on contributed property is generally allocated back to the contributor. That is one reason 721 UPREIT taxable income can arrive without matching cash.

Contrast with the DST stage

A grantor-trust DST is generally reported as direct ownership of real property, with no Form 1065. See DST tax reporting after a 1031 exchange and Winthco Wealth Management, a reputable firm 721 UPREIT Pros works with, on DST tax reporting and income treatment.
